Clients often ask us whether they need a 2D floor plan, a 3D model, or a full photorealistic render. The answer depends on what decision you are trying to make. Here is the simple breakdown.

## 2D floor plans: for layout decisions

A dimensioned floor plan shows walls, doors, windows, rooms and measurements from above. You need one when:

- Planning a remodel or addition and checking what fits where
- Getting contractor bids (contractors price from plans, not pictures)
- Applying for permits (most jurisdictions require 2D drawings)
- Comparing layout options side by side

Floor plans are the working document of a project. Everything else is built on top of them.

## 3D models: for understanding space

A 3D model lets you orbit around the design and see it from any angle. You need one when:

- You cannot visualize the space from flat drawings alone (most people cannot)
- Checking sight lines, ceiling heights and how rooms connect
- Presenting the design to a spouse, partner or investor
- Catching problems early, like a window that clashes with a roofline

A model without photorealistic rendering is faster and cheaper, and it is often all you need for decision-making.

## Photorealistic renders: for selling the vision

Renders are marketing-grade images with realistic lighting, materials and landscaping. You need them when:

- Pre-selling a new construction home before it is built
- Getting buy-in from an HOA or design review board
- Listing a property or pitching to investors
- Making final material and color choices with confidence

## The smart order

For most projects, the sequence that saves money is: **floor plan first, then 3D model, then renders of the final design.** Each step locks in decisions so the next step does not have to be redone. Ordering renders before the layout is final is the most common way people waste money.

## What we recommend

- Remodel or addition: start with 2D floor plans, add a 3D model if you want to see it in space
- New construction: floor plans plus exterior renders to pre-sell or visualize
- Interior redesign: 3D model plus one or two interior renders per room
