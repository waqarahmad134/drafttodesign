Your home's exterior is the first impression it makes, and for buyers it is the first filter. Studies consistently show that curb appeal can add 5 to 10 percent to a home's perceived value, and a thoughtful exterior makeover is often the highest-ROI project a homeowner can tackle. The good news is that most of the transformation comes from design decisions, not demolition.

Whether you are selling, refreshing a tired facade, or just tired of driving up to a house you do not love, these 10 exterior makeover ideas deliver the biggest visual impact for the money. Each one includes realistic 2026 cost ranges and the design thinking behind why it works.

## 1. Refresh the paint with a modern palette

Nothing changes an exterior faster than a new paint job. If your siding, stucco, or brick still looks dated, it is usually the color, not the material, that is aging it.

Modern 2026 exterior palettes lean toward warm whites, soft greige, deep charcoal accents, and muted earth tones. One of the strongest looks right now is a light warm-white body with a dark contrasting trim, or a deep moody color (navy, forest green, charcoal) softened by natural wood accents. If you have red or orange brick you do not want to paint, limewash or a mineral stain can tone it down without hiding the texture.

**Typical cost:** $5,000 to $15,000 for a full exterior repaint on a 2,000 sq ft home, depending on prep work and region. A two-tone scheme adds roughly 10 to 15 percent.

**Designer tip:** Test large swatches on different sides of the house and look at them in morning and evening light before committing. Colors shift dramatically outside.

## 2. Upgrade the front entry: door, hardware, and sidelights

The front door is the focal point of your facade. Buyers and guests notice it within seconds. A bold, modern entry door instantly signals that the rest of the house has been thought through.

Popular 2026 directions: oversized pivot doors, dark steel-and-glass styles, classic wood tones with long vertical pulls, and rich accent colors like deep teal or matte black. Add a modern house number plaque, a new mailbox, and sleek sconce lighting on both sides of the door.

**Typical cost:** $2,500 to $8,000 installed for a quality fiberglass or steel entry door; custom wood or pivot doors run $8,000 to $15,000+. According to Remodeling magazine's Cost vs. Value data, a steel entry door replacement consistently recoups a very high share of its cost at resale.

## 3. Add layered exterior lighting

Exterior lighting is the single most underrated curb-appeal upgrade. A house that looks flat at night can look architectural after dark with the right plan.

Work in three layers: **accent lighting** (uplights on trees and architectural features), **path lighting** (low bollards or well lights along walkways), and **entry lighting** (sconces, pendant lights at the porch).

**Typical cost:** $1,500 to $5,000 for a professionally installed landscape lighting package; DIY path lights cost $50 to $150 each.

## 4. Update windows, shutters, and trim details

You rarely need to replace every window to change the facade. The trim and detailing around them matter almost as much.

Consider wider, cleaner trim profiles (a modern 5.5-inch flat casing beats a skinny traditional one) or painting the window trim in a contrasting color. If shutters stay, size them correctly: each shutter should be half the window's width. Fake, too-narrow shutters are one of the most common curb-appeal killers.

**Typical cost:** New exterior trim details run $2,000 to $6,000 for a whole facade. Full window replacement is $8,000 to $25,000+ depending on count and type.

## 5. Rebuild or refresh the driveway and walkway

Cracked, oil-stained concrete drags the whole property down. The driveway is often the largest surface people see, so its condition matters.

Options by budget: a professional pressure wash and reseal ($300 to $800) buys several more years from tired concrete; a concrete overlay or stencil pattern refreshes the look for $3,000 to $8,000; full replacement with pavers, exposed aggregate, or stamped concrete runs $8,000 to $25,000. While you are at it, widen a narrow walkway to at least 4 feet so two people can walk side by side, and swap the straight contractor path for a gentle curve with flanking planting beds.

## 6. Modernize the porch and entry steps

Porches have made a huge comeback. Even a small covered stoop can be transformed with a few moves: wider steps (at least 4 feet), a clean modern railing in black metal or horizontal cable, a new porch ceiling in beadboard or stained wood, and a statement pendant light. If your porch columns look dated, replace chunky square columns with slimmer tapered ones, or wrap them in stained cedar for warmth.

For homes without a porch, a well-designed pergola or portico over the entry gives the facade depth and a sense of arrival.

**Typical cost:** Porch refreshes range from $2,000 to $10,000; adding a new portico or pergola runs $5,000 to $20,000.

## 7. Landscape with structure, not just plants

Great landscaping is 80 percent structure. Before buying a single plant, define the beds: generous curved beds at least 5 to 6 feet deep, a clean lawn edge, and a mulched border along every walkway. Then layer: evergreen anchors at the corners, mid-height shrubs, perennials for seasonal color, and groundcover to knit it together.

Stick to plants suited to your USDA zone so the landscape looks good in August, not just in May. A pair of statement planters flanking the entry finishes the composition.

**Typical cost:** Professional landscape design plus installation for a front yard runs $4,000 to $20,000. A DIY bed-and-mulch refresh can be done for $500 to $2,000.

## 8. Hide the clutter: fences, screens, and storage

Nothing kills curb appeal like visible trash cans, AC units, and utility meters. Simple cedar or composite screens around utilities cost a few hundred dollars and instantly tidy the side of the house. A new mailbox post, coordinated house numbers, and one cohesive outdoor trash enclosure can make the same difference as a much bigger project.

Fencing is the bigger-ticket version of this idea: a horizontal-slat cedar fence or a clean black metal fence reframes the whole property line and reads as modern instantly.

**Typical cost:** Utility screens $200 to $800; new fencing $15 to $45 per linear foot installed, depending on material.

## 9. Add architectural character: gables, cladding, and millwork

If your facade is flat and featureless, the fix is architectural detail, not just paint. The highest-impact moves:

- **Mixed materials:** pair stucco or siding with one accent band of wood, stone veneer, or board-and-batten. One accent wall is enough.
- **Gable detailing:** add decorative gable vents, brackets, or a contrasting gable color to give the roofline interest.
- **Modern millwork:** a clean pergola beam over the garage, a slatted wood screen on the porch, or a new modern garage door (a huge surface that is often ignored).

A garage door replacement deserves special mention: garage doors can cover 30 percent or more of the front facade, and swapping a plain white panel door for a modern carriage-style or glass-and-aluminum door is one of the highest-ROI single upgrades you can make.

**Typical cost:** Garage door replacement $1,500 to $5,000. Mixed-material accent cladding $5,000 to $15,000 depending on area.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does a full exterior makeover cost in 2026?

A light cosmetic refresh (paint touch-ups, landscaping, lighting, entry upgrades) typically runs **$5,000 to $20,000**. A mid-range makeover (full repaint, new door, garage door, landscaping overhaul) lands around **$20,000 to $50,000**. A full facade transformation with new cladding, porch work, and driveway replacement can exceed **$75,000**.

### Which exterior upgrade adds the most home value?

Exterior paint, a new entry door, and garage door replacement consistently rank among the highest-ROI projects in Remodeling's Cost vs. Value report. Curb appeal improvements broadly can lift perceived home value by **5 to 10 percent**, which on a $400,000 home is $20,000 to $40,000.

### Should I paint my brick house?

Painting brick is permanent and can trap moisture if done wrong, so it is a commitment. If you love the look of painted brick (and most modern palettes do), use a breathable mineral paint or limewash and hire a painter experienced with masonry. If the brick is in good shape and just looks dated, a limewash is more forgiving than full paint.

### How do I choose exterior colors that won't look dated in 5 years?

Stick to timeless neutrals (warm whites, greige, charcoal, classic navy) for the big surfaces and put trends in replaceable elements like the door color and planters. A two-material facade (e.g., painted siding plus natural wood) ages better than an all-in-one bold color.
