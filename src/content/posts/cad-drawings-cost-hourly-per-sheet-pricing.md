If you need a floor plan drawn up, a set of remodel plans for a contractor, or drawings for a permit application, the first question is always the same: how much do CAD drawings cost? The answer depends on what you are drawing, how it is priced, and who is doing the work. Here is an honest breakdown of hourly and per-sheet pricing for 2026 so you can budget realistically.

## The short answer: typical CAD drawing costs

- **Freelance CAD drafter, hourly:** $30 to $75 per hour. Offshore drafters can be $15 to $30 per hour, while senior US-based drafters with specialty experience charge $75 to $125 per hour.
- **Simple single drawing (one floor plan or elevation):** $100 to $500 per sheet.
- **Residential remodel plan set (4 to 8 sheets):** $600 to $2,500 per set.
- **Full new-construction house plan set (10 to 20 sheets):** $2,000 to $7,000+ per set, depending on home size and detail level.

Most residential CAD work is billed per sheet or per project, not hourly. Hourly billing shows up mostly in long-term contract work, complex revisions, or consulting-style engagements where the scope is hard to define upfront.

## Hourly pricing: how it works and when it makes sense

Hourly rates for CAD drafters vary mainly by location, experience, and specialty. A junior drafter doing straightforward 2D linework bills at the low end. A senior drafter who can produce permit-ready sets, coordinate with structural engineers, and handle complex geometries bills at the high end.

| Drafter type | Typical hourly rate |
|---|---|
| Offshore / entry-level freelancer | $15 to $30 |
| US-based junior drafter | $30 to $50 |
| US-based experienced drafter | $50 to $75 |
| Senior / specialty drafter (MEP, structural, complex commercial) | $75 to $125 |
| Licensed architect doing CAD work | $100 to $250 |

Hourly billing makes sense when the scope is open-ended: ongoing revisions, as-built documentation of a large building, or design development where nobody can yet say how many sheets the job will take. The risk with hourly billing is on your side, a slow drafter or an unclear scope can inflate the bill. The fix is to ask for a written time estimate with a cap, and progress updates at agreed milestones.

## Per-sheet pricing: how it works and when it makes sense

Per-sheet pricing is the standard for defined deliverables. You agree on the list of sheets, and each sheet has a price based on its complexity. A simple dimensioned floor plan costs less than a detailed reflected ceiling plan with lighting schedules.

Typical per-sheet prices for residential work:

- **As-built floor plan (single floor):** $100 to $300 per sheet
- **Proposed floor plan (remodel or addition):** $150 to $400 per sheet
- **Elevations (front, rear, sides):** $100 to $250 per sheet
- **Sections:** $100 to $250 per sheet
- **Roof plan:** $75 to $200 per sheet
- **Electrical or reflected ceiling plan:** $100 to $300 per sheet
- **Site plan:** $100 to $300 per sheet
- **Detail sheets (stairs, kitchens, bathrooms):** $75 to $200 per sheet

A full remodel set usually combines 4 to 8 of these sheets, which is why most remodel plan sets land in the $600 to $2,500 range. Per-sheet pricing protects you because the deliverable is defined: you know exactly which drawings you get for the price.

## What affects the price of CAD drawings

**Project size and complexity.** A 1,200 sq ft rectangle is fast to draw. A 5,000 sq ft multi-level home with angled walls, vaulted ceilings, and custom staircases takes much longer. Complexity matters more than raw square footage.

**Quality of your source material.** Accurate field measurements, clear sketches with all dimensions, or existing drawings you can hand over all reduce drafting time. A shoebox of blurry photos and "the room is about 12 feet" adds hours of interpretation, and those hours show up in the price.

**Level of detail.** A basic wall layout with dimensions is quick. Add furniture layouts, electrical outlets, lighting plans, finish schedules, and construction notes, and each layer adds time. Be clear about what you actually need before you order.

**Revisions.** Most drafting services include one to three revision rounds. Extra rounds are typically billed hourly. The cheapest way to keep the price down is to finalize your decisions before the first draft, not during revisions.

**Turnaround speed.** Standard delivery is 3 to 7 business days per sheet set. Need it in 24 to 48 hours? Expect a rush fee of 25 to 50 percent. If you are not in a hurry, say so, it usually costs less.

**Permit-ready vs conceptual.** Drawings for a contractor's quote are lighter than drawings a building department will accept. Permit sets need code-compliant notes, correct sheet organization, and sometimes a professional stamp. Ask whether your project needs permit-grade drawings.

## Hourly vs per-sheet: which should you choose?

For most homeowners and small builders, per-sheet or per-project pricing wins. You get a fixed price, a defined deliverable, and no surprises. It aligns incentives: the drafter wants to finish efficiently, and you know what you are paying.

Hourly is better when you genuinely do not know the scope yet: documenting an entire existing building, iterating on a design with weekly changes, or supporting a project team over months. If you go hourly, protect yourself with a not-to-exceed cap and weekly time reports.

One hybrid that works well: fixed per-sheet price for the initial drawing set, hourly for any revisions beyond the included rounds. You get cost certainty on the main deliverable and a fair mechanism for scope creep.

## How to get an accurate quote

Send the drafter everything you have before asking for a price: measurements, sketches, photos, existing drawings, and a plain-English description of what each sheet should show. Vague requests get vague quotes, which turn into surprise change orders later.

Ask these questions before you commit:

1. Is this per sheet, per project, or hourly, and what exactly is included?
2. How many revision rounds are included, and what does each extra round cost?
3. What is the delivery timeline, and is there a rush option?
4. Are these drawings suitable for permits, contractor bids, or both?
5. What file formats will I receive (PDF, DWG, both)?

A professional drafting service will answer all five without hesitation. If a drafter cannot tell you what is included, walk away.

## Red flags in CAD pricing

Prices that seem too good to be true usually are. A $50 full plan set is almost certainly a template with your address pasted in, not a drawing of your home. Other warning signs: no written agreement, no revision policy, no sample work, and anyone who asks for full payment upfront. A typical structure is 50 percent deposit and 50 percent on delivery, or milestone payments for larger sets.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does a single CAD floor plan cost?

A single dimensioned floor plan for one floor of a typical home costs $100 to $400 in 2026, depending on size, complexity, and the quality of your source measurements. As-built plans (documenting what exists) sit at the lower end; proposed remodel layouts with changes and notes sit at the higher end.

### Is it cheaper to hire a drafter hourly or per sheet?

Per sheet is usually cheaper and safer for defined work like a floor plan or a remodel set, because the price is fixed. Hourly can work out cheaper only if the drafter is fast and the scope is small, but it carries the risk of overruns. Always ask for a time cap with hourly work.

### What file formats should I receive?

At minimum, get a PDF for printing and sharing with contractors. The native CAD file (DWG or DXF) is worth requesting too, it lets you or another drafter edit the drawings later without redrawing from scratch. Some services charge extra for native files; confirm before you order.

### Do I need a licensed architect, or is a CAD drafter enough?

For most residential remodels, additions, and conceptual design, a skilled drafter is enough. You need a licensed architect or engineer when the work requires a professional stamp: new construction in most jurisdictions, structural changes, and anything your building department requires stamped drawings for. A good [drafting service](/services/architectural-drafting/) will tell you honestly when your project crosses that line.

### How long does it take to get CAD drawings done?

A single floor plan typically takes 2 to 4 business days. A full residential remodel set takes 1 to 2 weeks. Full new-construction sets take 2 to 4 weeks. Rush service can cut these timelines in half at a 25 to 50 percent premium.
