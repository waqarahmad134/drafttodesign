One of the most common questions homeowners ask before starting an interior design project is also one of the hardest to answer simply: how long will this actually take? The answer depends on the scope, the decisions involved, and, more than anything, the ordering lead times that sit outside anyone's control.

This guide lays out realistic 2026 timelines for every kind of interior design project, from a single-room refresh to a whole-home renovation. It breaks the process into phases so you can see where the time really goes, and it shows you exactly how to keep your project from stretching months beyond schedule.

## The short answer

For a quick reference:

- **Single room refresh (decorating only):** 4 to 8 weeks
- **Single room with renovation (kitchen or bath):** 8 to 16 weeks
- **Whole home, design concept only:** 6 to 12 weeks
- **Whole home, full-service with furnishings:** 6 to 12 months
- **Full home renovation with interior design:** 9 to 18 months

Notice that the design concept itself is rarely the slow part. A professional designer can develop a full concept for a single room in one to two weeks. What stretches timelines is procurement: furniture, custom pieces, and construction materials arriving on wildly different schedules.

## The 5 phases of an interior design project

Every interior design project, from a bedroom refresh to a full renovation, moves through the same five phases. Understanding them is the key to understanding where delays come from.

### Phase 1: Discovery and measurement (1 to 2 weeks)

The designer visits your home (or works remotely from your photos and floor plans), takes measurements, discusses how you use the space, and agrees on budget and style direction. If you already have [accurate 2D floor plans](/services/2d-floor-plans), this phase goes faster, because the designer starts with precise dimensions instead of hand measurements.

This phase usually takes 1 to 2 weeks, mostly waiting for calendar availability on both sides.

### Phase 2: Concept and design development (2 to 6 weeks)

This is the creative heart of the project. The designer develops the layout, color palette, materials, and furnishings, then presents the concept. Typical turnaround:

- Single room: 1 to 3 weeks for the first concept
- Whole home: 4 to 6 weeks, often presented room by room

Most designers include one or two revision rounds, each adding about a week. Projects stall here when feedback is slow or the direction changes mid-stream, so commit to a direction and refine it rather than restarting.

A [photorealistic 3D rendering](/services/3d-rendering) of the proposed design is common in this phase. It adds a few days to the timeline but can save weeks later, because seeing the space in 3D eliminates the miscommunications that cause costly changes during ordering or construction.

### Phase 3: Procurement and ordering (2 to 12 weeks, in parallel)

Once the design is approved, everything gets ordered: furniture, lighting, rugs, window treatments, fabrics, and materials. This is where most projects slow down, because lead times vary enormously:

- **In-stock furniture:** ships in 1 to 3 weeks
- **Made-to-order sofas and sectionals:** 8 to 14 weeks
- **Custom upholstery:** 10 to 16 weeks
- **Custom cabinetry and millwork:** 8 to 16 weeks
- **Custom rugs:** 12 to 20 weeks
- **Window treatments (custom):** 6 to 10 weeks
- **Lighting fixtures:** 2 to 8 weeks depending on the maker

Smart designers order the longest-lead items first, sometimes even before the design is fully finalized, to keep the critical path short. If your sofa needs 14 weeks and everything else needs 4, the sofa sets the project timeline.

### Phase 4: Construction or renovation work (varies, often 4 to 24 weeks)

For projects that involve remodeling, the contractor phase runs its own timeline:

- **Bathroom remodel:** 4 to 8 weeks of active work
- **Kitchen remodel:** 8 to 16 weeks of active work
- **Whole-home renovation:** 6 to 12 months of active work

Add 2 to 4 weeks before work starts for permits, which many cities require for plumbing, electrical, or structural changes. Permit timelines vary wildly by municipality, from same-week approvals in some suburbs to months in major cities with strict review.

If you need [architectural drafting](/services/architectural-drafting) for permit drawings, that typically takes 1 to 3 weeks and happens before the permit application goes in.

### Phase 5: Installation, styling, and reveal (1 to 3 weeks)

The final stretch: furniture delivery, installation of window treatments and lighting, hanging art, and styling the shelves. The designer usually needs one to three site visits. Punch-list fixes or a damaged item can add a week or two, which is why most designers build a small buffer into the schedule.

## Realistic timelines by project type

### Single room refresh (decorating only, no construction)

**Typical total: 4 to 8 weeks.**

Paint, new furniture, rugs, curtains, and styling. The concept takes 1 to 3 weeks, and furniture arrives in 1 to 6 weeks. Ordering the big pieces on day one makes the biggest difference. With everything in stock, a living room refresh can finish in under a month.

### Kitchen or bathroom design (with renovation)

**Typical total: 8 to 16 weeks for design and construction, plus lead time.**

Kitchens and baths are technical: cabinet layouts, plumbing positions, tile quantities, and lighting plans all have to be exact. Design runs 3 to 6 weeks, custom cabinets take 8 to 16 weeks to arrive, and construction takes 8 to 16 weeks for a kitchen or 4 to 8 for a bath. Finalize cabinet orders while decorative details are still being refined, so the 12-week cabinet clock is already running when construction starts.

### Whole home, design concept only (e-design or remote)

**Typical total: 6 to 12 weeks.**

Remote design services work room by room and deliver a shopping list you execute yourself. A 3-bedroom home usually takes 6 to 12 weeks from consultation to final list, depending on revision rounds.

### Whole home, full-service with furnishings

**Typical total: 6 to 12 months.**

This is the classic designer project: concept through installation. Expect 4 to 6 weeks of design development, then 4 to 10 months of ordering, receiving, and installing. Custom pieces and freight shipping set the pace.

### Full home renovation with interior design

**Typical total: 9 to 18 months.**

Design (6 to 12 weeks) overlaps with permitting (2 to 8 weeks), construction runs 6 to 12 months, and furnishings arrive toward the end. These projects have the most moving parts, so build in a 10 to 15 percent time buffer. Something always slips: a back-ordered tile, a permit revision, a subcontractor scheduling gap.

## 7 things that delay interior design projects

1. **Slow decision-making.** Every revision round adds about a week. Decide quickly, or limit yourself to one structured revision per room.
2. **Long-lead custom pieces.** Custom sofas, rugs, and cabinetry can take 12 to 20 weeks. Order them first.
3. **Permit delays.** Check your city's average review time before scheduling construction.
4. **Changing direction mid-project.** Restarting the concept after ordering has begun wastes both time and money.
5. **Back-ordered items.** Designers should confirm stock or realistic ship dates before presenting options.
6. **Contractor scheduling gaps.** Good contractors book 2 to 3 months out. Lock yours in early.
7. **DIY installation bottlenecks.** If you are executing an e-design yourself, schedule delivery and assembly help in advance.

## How to speed up your project without cutting corners

- **Get floor plans ready first.** Precise measurements on day one eliminate the measuring phase and let the designer start immediately. Our [2D floor plan service](/services/2d-floor-plans) turns your space into accurate plans a designer can work from.
- **Approve long-lead items early.** Ask your designer to identify the longest-lead pieces in the first presentation and approve those first.
- **Visualize before you buy.** A [3D rendering of your space](/services/3d-rendering) catches mistakes that cost weeks to fix after delivery.
- **Choose in-stock where it does not matter.** Custom is worth it for hero pieces like the sofa; in-stock is fine for side tables and accessories, and it ships in days.
- **Batch your decisions.** One focused decision meeting per week keeps momentum better than scattered daily messages.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does it take to design a single bedroom?

A bedroom design concept typically takes 1 to 2 weeks, and the full project, including furniture delivery and installation, runs 4 to 8 weeks. Bedrooms are straightforward because they rarely involve construction; the timeline is almost entirely set by furniture lead times.

### Can interior design be done in a month?

Yes, for a single room with in-stock furniture and no construction, a one-month turnaround is realistic. The design concept takes a week or two, and in-stock pieces ship within 1 to 3 weeks. Custom pieces, renovations, or permits will push any project past a month.

### How long does furniture delivery take in 2026?

In-stock furniture: 1 to 3 weeks. Made-to-order sofas and sectionals: 8 to 14 weeks. Custom upholstery: 10 to 16 weeks. Custom rugs: 12 to 20 weeks. These are the numbers that set real project timelines, so always confirm lead times before falling in love with a piece.

### What is the longest part of an interior design project?

Procurement, the ordering and receiving of furniture and materials, is almost always the longest phase. The design concept takes weeks; waiting for a custom sofa or custom cabinetry takes months. That is why ordering long-lead items early is the single most effective schedule strategy.

### How long does a kitchen design take from start to finish?

Design development takes 3 to 6 weeks, custom cabinets need 8 to 16 weeks to arrive, and construction runs 8 to 16 weeks. A realistic total is 4 to 8 months from the first designer meeting to a finished kitchen, assuming permits move smoothly.

### Should I hire the designer before or after the contractor?

Hire the designer first. The design determines what the contractor builds, and starting construction without finalized plans is the most expensive mistake in renovation. Finalize the design, then bring the contractor in to bid on a complete scope.
